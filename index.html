Retail services · Time series02

Forecasting demand. Planning with confidence.

Translating salon sales into practical decisions about stock, staffing and campaigns.

MONTHLY FORECAST RANGER$8–12k
Apr 2025–Mar 2026 · ARIMA forecast
17 monthsof daily point-of-sale data

ARIMA · Prophet · Data preparation

Explore case study +
Business question
When will home-care product demand peak, so the salon can time promotions, staff and stock?
Data preparation
Daily sales came from the salon’s point-of-sale system (date, product, category, value, quantity). Category labels were inconsistent (for example CAB, CABELEIREIRO, CABELEREIRO, HOME CARE and Home Care), so I grouped them into hair-care and home-care sales and aggregated them into a monthly series.
Method
I used two models because the questions work on different time scales. Prophet splits daily sales into trend and weekly seasonality. ARIMA models the short monthly series and gives a forecast with confidence intervals.
Key findings
Friday and Saturday are consistently strongest, and Tuesday is weakest. The monthly forecast peaks around August and September 2025 (about R$11.9k and R$12.0k) and dips in June and October (about R$8.8k and R$8.9k). The bands are wide, about R$2k to R$3k either side, so I present them as ranges, not exact targets.
Business decision
Run home-care campaigns and bundles on Fridays and Saturdays and in the forecast peak months, set staffing by weekday, and order less stock before the quiet months to avoid overstock. The goal is a higher average ticket per client.
Limits and next steps
There are only 17 monthly points, so a yearly cycle cannot be proven yet. My report does not include an error measure such as MAPE or RMSE on held-out months. That back-test is the first thing I would add, followed by promotion and holiday data, and product-level forecasts.

Customer segmentation · Model evaluation

Business question
Why do customers cancel, and who is likely to?
Key findings
Churn exceeded 10% for Basic and Free plans, compared with approximately 2% for Enterprise plans. One predictive model missed most actual cancellations, limiting its usefulness for retention decisions.
Recommendation
Prioritise retention initiatives for smaller accounts and customers on monthly contracts.
